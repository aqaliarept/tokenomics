# 7.22 proposal: a rule for "input arrived late" at the final assembler (on top of buy-ahead k = 4)

Status: **PROPOSAL, for the supervisor's approval before any code** (2026-11-05).

## What the data says
- **The size of the loss.** At 12 producers with MWWk, late inputs are ≈ 174k lost consumer units
  under `entity_planner`, 178k at k = 4 (EXP-0074 / EXP-0077). They are flat across k = 1–4 while
  every other input class falls. RR: 88k → 90k.
- **Class definition** (`b2_late`, eval/supply_trace.py): a reservation of the assembler failed
  waiting for input g, while a replenishment order for g was live and was delivered in the failing
  cycle (after assembly) or later.
- **One-shot local probe** (`probes/late_probe.py`, not a reading; run from `tokenomics/` with the worlds as arguments). Buy-ahead k = 4 + MWWk, r0, one
  episode each of the two 12-producer worlds with the most late loss, 12-0254 and 12-0111. Every
  late unit was traced to its reservation and its late input order. By units:

  | measure | 12-0254 | 12-0111 |
  |---|---|---|
  | late input order placed after the reservation was accepted | 99.6% | 100% |
  | its lead time (created → delivered), median | 1 cycle | 1 cycle |
  | delivered after the reservation failed, median | 1 cycle | 1 cycle |
  | assembler's live headroom below the shortfall at failure | 42% | 77% |
  | headroom exactly 0 at failure | 23% | 26% |
  | units on the layer-2 (final) assemblers `p2_*` | ≈ 100% | ≈ 100% |

- **Reading of the probe:**
  - The final assembler orders the missing input only after it has reserved the order. A producer
    plans its command before the auction, so it cannot know which bids it will win; it reacts one
    cycle late. A 1-cycle lead then misses the 2-cycle reservation window by about one cycle.
  - In 42–77% of the late units, the order for the missing input could not be placed in full
    because the inventory headroom was smaller than the shortfall.
  - Buy-ahead fills headroom with stock of the inputs it used recently, so it crowds out the input
    a reservation is waiting for. That fits "late" staying flat (or rising) as k grows.
  - `entity_planner` requests an inventory expansion only at headroom exactly 0
    (`score_producer_entity_view`: `inventory_expansion_value` > 0 iff headroom ≤ 0). So the
    "headroom > 0 but < shortfall" cases never expand.

## What a producer can do (domain, ADR-0058)
- **Its only levers** are its `ProducingAgentCommand`: restoration / upgrade / inventory-expansion
  toggles and per-input replenishment units, planned from its own `ProducerEntityView`.
- **What it cannot do.** It cannot decline a bid (the auction is automatic), choose which
  reservation gets stock first, or speed a delivery (orderers route its input orders; the fund
  backs them).
- **What it can see, agent-local and current:**
  - per input g: `inventory`, `outstanding_inbound`, `blocked_reservation_qty` (units of g its live
    reservations still need) and `downstream_demand_pressure`;
  - `inventory_headroom`;
  - the running-investment flags;
  - its own completed-units EMA (past cycles).
  None of these is another agent's same-cycle routing.

## Proposed rule: `entity_planner_buyahead_k4_rf` ("reservations first")
Same as buy-ahead k = 4, with three changes to the replenishment step only:
1. **Blocked shortfall first.** Per input g, shortfall_g = max(0, `blocked_reservation_qty`_g −
   `inventory`_g − `outstanding_inbound`_g). The cycle's headroom (and the `max_replenish`
   ceiling) is spent on shortfall_g first, by input value, before any planner target or buy-ahead
   stock.
2. **Keep room for reservations.** The buy-ahead k-term (the stock above `entity_planner`'s
   target) may not use the last R units of headroom, where R = ⌈EMA of past cycles' total blocked
   shortfall⌉. That is an agent-local EMA (α 0.3) kept in the policy from its own views. With R = 0
   the rule reduces to "blocked first + buy-ahead".
3. **Expand earlier.** Request an inventory expansion (when configured, not running and fundable,
   as today) also when headroom < the total blocked shortfall, not only at headroom 0.

**Unchanged:** restoration, upgrade, the input-value priority among non-blocked wants, and k = 4.
- k = 4 with (1)–(3) off is `entity_planner_buyahead_k4` exactly (tested, as k = 0 == entity_planner).
- **Feasibility:** all three use only fields of the producer's own view. It is a policy in
  `eval/baselines.py` (`EXTRA_PRODUCING`); no `domain/` change.

**What it cannot fix:** orders whose input lead time exceeds the reservation window even when
ordered at once. Ordering is still one cycle after acceptance, because the producer cannot see bids
before the auction. The probe suggests most late inputs have a 1-cycle lead, which (1) and (2) can
bring inside the window.

## Implemented (2026-11-05, supervisor approved the full rule + observer additions)
- **The rule** (`eval/baselines.py`):
  - `plan_reservations_first` and `producing_entity_planner_buyahead_rf(k=4, blocked_first,
    keep_room, expand_early)`, all three parts as approved;
  - registered: `entity_planner_buyahead_k4_rf` (all three, deciding), `_k4_rf1` ((1) only) and
    `_k4_rf12` ((1) + (2)) as reported ablations;
  - all parts off == `entity_planner_buyahead_k4` (tested on a view and action for action over a
    composite episode). `entity_planner` and k = 1–4 are untouched.
- **Planning order:**
  - (1) the blocked shortfall, by input value, within headroom and the ceiling;
  - then `entity_planner`'s own target on top;
  - then the buy-ahead k-term on top, minus the kept room R (⌈EMA α 0.3 of past cycles' total
    blocked shortfall⌉, per (system, producer) in the policy, read before the current cycle
    enters it).
  - The fund caps the replenishment units after the planner's restoration choice. The upgrade is
    re-checked after the new replenishment spend, and expansion (3) after both.
- **The observer** (`eval/supply_trace.py`): added cells only. Every earlier cell is
  byte-identical (tested against EXP-0077's k = 4 rows on one world, horizon 100).
  - `st_b2_late_headroom_blocked`: the late units whose missing input still had an unordered
    shortfall beyond the headroom after the assembler's first command following the acceptance.
  - `st_producer_cycles_x`, which adds `headroom` and `expansion_placed` / `_completed` /
    `_rejected` to `producer_cycles.csv`.

## Correction to the probe above
- **The probe overstated headroom-blocking.** Its "headroom below the shortfall at failure"
  (42% / 77%) compared headroom with need − stock, ignoring inputs already inbound.
- **The observer's corrected definition:** the shortfall net of inbound, at the first command
  after acceptance. Under k = 4 it gives:
  - 12-0254: 1–4% of late units (82–416 of ≈ 10k per episode);
  - 12-0111: 58–69% (3.1–3.9k of ≈ 5.3–5.7k).
  So headroom matters on some worlds, not all.
- **The ordering delay stays the dominant mechanism:** the input is ordered after the acceptance
  and arrives about one cycle too late.
- **Smoke, the same two worlds, not a reading.** rf vs k = 4:
  - late units −5% to −17%;
  - headroom-blocked late −70% (0111) and ≈ −100% (0254);
  - consumer units +1% to +7% on 0111, mixed on 0254 (MWWk +1% to +13%, RR −10%).
